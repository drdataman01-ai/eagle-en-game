He Rose from the Dead

Before sunset, they took my Master’s body down from the cross.

A disciple named Joseph went to Pilate and asked for my Master’s body. With the help of several others, he washed the blood and wounds from my Master’s body. Then they wrapped Him in thick, white linen and laid Him in a new tomb.

Beside the entrance of the tomb was a great round stone, taller than a man. It was slightly higher than the opening and was meant to seal the tomb. They placed my Master’s body on the stone bed inside the tomb and came out. Then several men pushed together and rolled the great stone into place.

The entrance was sealed. A small company of Roman soldiers came to guard the tomb.

Roman soldiers always moved together. They never went alone. Each soldier wore a helmet and metal armor. They carried spears, short swords, and shields.

Several women who did not want to leave my Master remained nearby, watching the tomb from a distance.

Those who opposed my Master went to Pilate and told him that Jesus had said He would rise again after three days.

So they asked Pilate to order the soldiers to guard the tomb, lest Jesus’ disciples come and steal His body and then claim that He had risen from the dead.

I had seen my Master die with my own eyes.

His followers had seen Him die as well.

The soldiers themselves were certain that He had died upon the cross. That was why they had taken His body down.

You who opposed my Master, why were you so determined to guard His tomb? Did you believe in His resurrection more than we did?

I flew without knowing where I was going.

I flew over the places where my Master had once walked and taught. As I approached a narrow gate, I noticed words written above it. They had been written there by some of my Master’s disciples:

“Come to Me,
all you who are weary and burdened,
and I will give you rest.”

Those words brought great comfort to my Master’s disciples.

If my Master could endure such unbearable suffering, then surely He could comfort anyone who had suffered—or would ever suffer—the same kind of pain.

Centurion slowly came to the narrow gate and sat down beneath it. He bowed his head and remained silent for a long time.

“The whole earth has been cursed because of man,” he said quietly.

“The weight of our sin is heavier than we can carry.” He fell silent again and held his head in his hands.

“We are a burden upon the whole earth. The earth itself can hardly bear us.”

He lifted his head and looked toward the sky. His eyes were filled with weariness.

“Now we have even killed the Son of God.”

His voice began to tremble. “How will we ever escape the judgment that is to come? How will we escape the wrath of God?”

When he finished speaking, he said nothing for a long while. At last, leaning against the narrow gate, he fell into an exhausted sleep.

I watched him from nearby. I did not know what to say to him. So I flew back to the top of the cliff.

At the first light of dawn, I had not yet seen the sun. But the sky was already beginning to shine. The light came from the direction of the tomb.

A pillar of light rose from the earth and reached into the heavens. It grew brighter and brighter. The entire tomb seemed to be surrounded by light. Then, suddenly, I saw countless hosts of angels descending through the pillar of light.

They came down from the heavens as though the sky itself had opened, and they assembled in perfect order before the tomb. A mighty earthquake followed. One of the angelic warriors rolled the great stone away.

Two angels entered the tomb to wait for my Master. The great hosts of angels remained outside, standing in perfect ranks.

The tomb was filled with light. And then I saw my Master standing there. The linen cloths that had wrapped His body were still lying upon the stone bed.

My Master had risen. Light came forth from Him. It grew brighter and brighter until it shone more brilliantly than the light of the sun itself. I could hardly look at Him.

I could not believe what I was seeing. Only yesterday, I had watched Him die upon the cross. I had seen Him bow His head. I had seen Him breathe His last.

But now He was standing inside the tomb. He was alive.

My Master was alive.

He spoke to the angels waiting outside:

“I have someone I must go and see.”

He instructed the two leading angels to remain, while the rest of the heavenly host withdrew in perfect order toward the horizon.

Then Mary Magdalene came out from behind a tree. Weeping, she ran toward the tomb. She looked inside and saw two angels dressed in white. But my Master was no longer there. She saw Him, but she thought He was the gardener. Through her tears, she cried:

“They have taken my Lord away, and I do not know where they have put Him.”

Then my Master called her by the voice she knew so well.

“Mary.”

Her eyes opened wide. She recognized Him. So that was the person my Master had said He was going to see.

Mary Magdalene.

My Master said to her:

“Do not hold on to Me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father.”

“Go instead to My brothers and tell them, ‘I am ascending to My Father and your Father, to My God and your God.’”

Mary Magdalene could hardly believe what she had seen. Tears of overwhelming joy filled her face. She ran to tell the other disciples everything. As she ran, she kept crying out:

“He is alive! He is alive!”

I flew excitedly above her. I had seen it with my own eyes. I had seen how my Master had risen from the dead.

As Mary approached the village and began telling the other disciples, I remembered Centurion, still sitting beneath the narrow gate.

Centurion did not know that my Master had risen from the dead. He did not know that the judgment he had feared the night before was no longer the same. I decided to turn back and tell him the good news.

I flew to the narrow gate. Centurion was still asleep. I woke him with a sharp cry. He seemed to understand that I had something important to tell him. He stood up and followed me. I flew ahead, leading him toward the place where Mary Magdalene and the other disciples had gathered. I flew in front. Centurion followed behind. He still did not know where I was taking him. But I knew.

I was going to show him what I had seen!

I was going to show him glory!

My Master was alive!
